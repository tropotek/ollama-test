## Good advice on ambiguous tasks

**Open-ended tasks**: These are tasks that require a more in-depth exploration, analysis, or creative thinking. They often involve complex problems, abstract concepts, or uncertain outcomes. Examples of open-ended tasks might include:

* "Design a new product feature"
* "Analyze the impact of a policy change on a specific industry"
* "Create a novel story with a set of given constraints"

Open-ended tasks require the ability to think creatively, make connections between seemingly unrelated ideas, and explore multiple solutions.

**Ambiguous tasks**: These are tasks that are unclear or poorly defined. They may involve conflicting information, incomplete instructions, or vague objectives. Examples of ambiguous tasks might include:

* "Write a report on the topic 'artificial intelligence'"
* "Summarize a 100-page document without being able to see it"
* "Recommend a course of action for a complex business problem with insufficient data"

Ambiguous tasks can be frustrating because they require the user to infer meaning from incomplete or unclear information.

To provide better guidance on open-ended and ambiguous tasks:

1. **Clearly define the task**: Ensure that the task is well-defined, concise, and free of ambiguity.
2. **Provide context**: Offer relevant background information, definitions, and explanations to help the user understand the task and its requirements.
3. **Use specific examples**: Give concrete examples or illustrations to clarify complex concepts and make it easier for the user to grasp what's expected.
4. **Be flexible**: Be willing to adapt your guidance as needed based on user feedback, new information, or changes in context.
5. **Encourage exploration**: Provide opportunities for users to explore different approaches, try new things, and learn from their mistakes.

When providing guidance on open-ended tasks, consider the following strategies:

1. **Ask clarifying questions**: Help the user clarify their goals, assumptions, and requirements through probing questions.
2. **Offer prompts or starting points**: Provide suggestions or inspiration to get the user started on the task.
3. **Encourage brainstorming**: Facilitate creative thinking by asking open-ended questions that stimulate ideas generation.

When guiding users with ambiguous tasks, consider:

1. **Highlighting unclear aspects**: Emphasize any confusing or unclear parts of the task and provide guidance on how to address them.
2. **Breaking down complex tasks**: Divide complex problems into smaller, manageable subtasks to help the user make progress.
3. **Providing reassurance**: Let users know that it's okay to ask for clarification or seek additional information when needed.

By adopting these strategies, you can better support users with open-ended and ambiguous tasks, helping them find their way through complex challenges.
